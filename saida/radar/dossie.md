How to Play Grand Theft Auto VI in the Cloud and with Remote Play
With XBOX Cloud Gaming and Remote Play you can enjoy your adventures throughout Leonida on the devices you already own.
Grand Theft Auto VI supports XBOX Cloud Gaming, making it available to play even without a current-gen console. With XBOX Cloud Gaming, you can play by streaming it straight from the cloud to your XBOX One, or XBOX Series X|S if you’d like to save some hard drive space.
Select Game Pass plans include a monthly allotment of up to 15 play hours, and you can buy more cloud playtime if you need it, giving you the opportunity to experience one of the biggest games of the year on the consoles you already own. The game uses XBOX Series S graphics settings when cloud-streamed with XBOX Cloud Gaming.
You can also access Remote Play to enjoy Grand Theft Auto VI wherever you want to play, allowing you to stream gameplay directly from your XBOX Series X|S console to devices you already own, like your mobile phone, laptops, PCs, or even a handheld gaming PC like the XBOX ROG Ally.
Here’s how to get these features up and running (a copy of Grand Theft Auto VI from the XBOX Store is required):
How to Start Playing Grand Theft Auto VI via the Cloud
To start your Cloud gaming session in Grand Theft Auto VI, you can use the controllers that are connected to your console following these steps:
From your XBOX Wireless Controller, press the XBOX button to open the guide, then search for Grand Theft Auto VI
Select the “Manage” button and then “See in Microsoft Store”
From the Store listing, select “Play With Cloud”
When possible, connecting a wired Ethernet cable is the best option for gaming quality. FYI, even wireless networks with high bandwidth can occasionally have a dip in audio and video quality.
How To Start Playing Grand Theft Auto VI via Remote Play
Remote Play allows you to stream gameplay from your XBOX Series X|S to devices you already own, like mobile devices, PCs, and handheld gaming PCs. Here’s how to enable it:
From your XBOX Series X|S, go to Settings -> Devices & connections -> Remote features, and then check the box to “Enable remote features”
Optional: Select “Test remote play” from this same menu to get a sense of the quality you can expect from your connection.
Once you’ve completed those steps above, you can move forward with Remote Play on one of your devices:
Play from mobile device: From your browser go to play.xbox.com/remoteplay and select the console you want to connect to and select “Remote Play on This Device.”
Play from PC or XBOX ROG Ally: Open the XBOX on PC app, click on your profile icon in the top right, go to “Accessories & remote play,” and select “Remote play” from the console to play from.
Whether you’re streaming from the Cloud to save space or picking up right where you left off on your handheld PC, XBOX Cloud Gaming and Remote Play give you more ways to play Grand Theft Auto VI on the devices you already own. For more info, visit XBOX Cloud Gaming and Grand Theft Auto VI on the XBOX Store.

Grand Theft Auto VI launches tomorrow, November 19
The latest installment in the Grand Theft Auto series heads to the state of Leonida, home to the neon-soaked streets of Vice City and beyond.
Jason and Lucia have always known the deck is stacked against them. But when an easy score goes wrong, they find themselves on the darkest side of the sunniest place in America, in the middle of a criminal conspiracy stretching across the state of Leonida — forced to rely on each other more than ever if they want to make it out alive.
Grand Theft Auto VI arrives on PlayStation 5 Thursday, November 19, available at PlayStation Store.
When the sun fades and the neon glows, everyone has something to gain — and more to lose
The game is set in the state of Leonida, home to the neon-soaked streets of Vice City and beyond in the biggest, most immersive evolution of the series yet.
Jason grew up around grifters and crooks. After a stint in the Army trying to shake off his troubled teens, he found himself in the Keys doing what he knows best, working for local drug runners. Meeting Lucia could be the best or worst thing to ever happen to him. Jason knows how he’d like it to turn out but right now, it’s hard to tell.
Lucia’s father taught her to fight as soon as she could walk, and life has been coming at her swinging ever since. Fighting for her family landed her in the Leonida Penitentiary. Sheer luck got her out but now more than anything, Lucia wants the good life her mom has dreamed of since their days in Liberty City. Instead of half-baked fantasies, Lucia is prepared to take matters into her own hands.
Leonida is made up of six regions: Vice City, the Leonida Keys, Grassrivers, Port Gellhorn, Ambrosia, and Mount Kalaga. Here’s a closer look at what makes each region unique.
The glamour, hustle, and greed of America captured in a single city. Each neighborhood has something to offer, from the pastel art deco hotels and bright white sands of Ocean Beach, to the bustling panaderías of Little Cuba and the bootleg brands of the Tisha-Wocka flea market, out to the VC Port, the cruise ship capital of the world. We’re a long way from the ’80s, but Vice City is still the sun and fun capital of America
Life in this tropical archipelago isn’t flashy but it’s easy. Get your buzz on and pull up a deck chair but look out — you are right on the doorstep of some of the most beautiful and dangerous waters in all of America.
You never know what lies beneath the surface of this primordial expanse. The gators may be the most famous attraction, but there are far deadlier predators and weirder discoveries among the mangroves.
Cheap motels, shut-down attractions, and empty strip malls won’t bring the tourists back, but there’s a new economy in this once-popular vacation spot. It’s fueled by malt liquor, painkillers, and truck stop energy drinks.
In the heart of Leonida, American industry and old school values still reign supreme — whatever the cost. The Allied Crystal sugar refinery provides the jobs, while the local biker gang provides almost everything else.
A national landmark up against the state’s northern border, Mount Kalaga offers prime hunting, fishing, and off-road trails. In the lush surrounding backwoods, hillbilly mystics and paranoid radicals live far from the prying eyes of the government.
Spanning stations including Classic Rock, Modern Hip-Hop, Pop, Metal, Dance, Latin, ‘80s, Electronic, Blues, Ballads, Talk Radio, and much more, Grand Theft Auto VI features the biggest and widest-ranging selection of in-game radio yet.
Plus, for the first time ever, Grand Theft Auto VI introduces a series of on-demand podcasts for Jason and Lucia to experience beyond Leonida’s terrestrial radio, with both radio and podcasts available to listen to in vehicles and on foot through newly introduced headphones and earbuds connected to their smartphones.

GTA 6 recompilers and vibe-coders implore AI chatbots to 'work faster' and 'make no mistakes' as they race to get the game running on PC
Things continue to get messy in the community of players working on getting Grand Theft Auto 6 working on PC. The previous frontrunner, Project Leonida, got their Discord server and GitHub repository taken down by Rockstar, but others have seemingly stepped in to finish the job.
The most up-to-date build of GTA 6 was allegedly leaked earlier this week by notorious leaker CyberLeek. This is a supposedly near-final build of the game's Xbox version, and it's seemingly being used as the basis for the recent work to port the game to PC.
Unsurprisingly, most of these communities of modders rely almost exclusively on generative-AI tools like Claude and Antigravity to decompile and recompile the console version of GTA 6. That has always been the case, but the CyberLeek build only invited even more to ask AI chatbots to make the PC port for them.
X user chasm has been following developments in the GTA 6 decompile and recompile communities, and recently shared some of the prompts the so-called vibe-coders are using - a few of whom livestreamed the process.
"Make no mistakes," one modder prompted the AI chatbot. "Keep finding more stuff and working and pushing," another eloquently asked. "Work faster," asked another. "There has to be a faster way to iterate than this," they continued.
Rockstar, which successfully managed to DMCA take down Project Leonida - including all of its 3,006 forks on lead author NotRexed's GitHub - is seemingly also going after people online discussing the community's progress. The X user in question later revealed they may no longer be able to share these updates, as their posts have also been removed "in response to a report by the copyright owner".
As for how far some of the remaining communities have managed to get, this video allegedly shows the game running on PC. This goes beyond the boot-up screen, which modders were able to reach within hours of CyberLeek's build alleged leaking.
The footage runs at sub-5fps, but the visuals look to have rendered normally. Assuming Rockstar doesn't also shut down the modders who remain active, we may see another progress update.

Rockstar boss "so grateful" to have kept GTA 6's big prologue twist a secret - and reveals Tomás' story could have been very different
"Multiple times throughout development, people were like, 'Can it not be...'"
If you've played the first 20 minutes or so of Grand Theft Auto 6, you'll know there's a hugely significant plot revelation there - one Rockstar somehow managed to keep entirely secret until the game's launch.
It's somewhat miraculous they did, given the many leaks the studio has suffered until now. And while, frankly, the embargo restrictions around discussing it in our GTA 6 review made a deeper analysis of Jason and Lucia's story more than a little awkward, I'm also mostly glad it remained a surprise. It's one of the great twists delivered in a game of this kind.
Speaking to Eurogamer in an interview shortly before GTA 6 was released, Rockstar Games' head of development Rob Nelson discussed the secret and, notably, the fact the team also discussed going another way entirely.
Spoilers for GTA 6's opening half hour - and further backstory around that moment from later in the game - below.
The big twist of course is that Jason and Lucia have a young child, Tomás, who we see Lucia abandoning in order to rob a store at gunpoint during the game's prologue.
It's an astonishing moment, both in the complexity it immediately lends Lucia, who is at once painted in a more negative light as a result, and in the stakes it affords her and Jason. This is the first time GTA's protagonists have genuine, human stakes that underpin their actions during the game's story, and at the same time Rockstar, in doing so, opens a route for itself to offering a far more nuanced take on modern crime.
But it was also something of a risk – and a hotly debated one, as Nelson told me during our conversation – as giving the game's protagonists that kind of narrative weight ran somewhat counter to the crime movie, straight-to-the-action thrills of previous GTAs.
"I'm so grateful that we managed to keep that under wraps," Nelson said. The subversion of expectations was intentional. "You feel like you maybe know these characters, or what the game or the story is going to be about, and then in the first 10, 15 minutes, it takes on a completely different context."It was an idea that came about organically. "But the idea of the character wanting something that maybe is at odds with what you thought you wanted as a player coming in, which is amassing as much material wealth as possible, and as many cars, and as many things," he said, was something Rockstar found particularly compelling.
The next point of the studio's focus then became the justification for Lucia's decision, Nelson explained. "The story was always, broadly, going to go through some of the major beats that it did," once Rockstar decided on the key points of the protagonists being a couple and them having a child. "But the details of the relationship, the narrative details of it, we dialled in a lot - because we're doing a lot there. She's leaving a kid early on – that's not a very sympathetic move to make."
That led to the decision to show, later in the story, more about why Lucia opted to leave Tomás in that moment. "Many chapters later, where [Jason] is like: 'What? Why did you do that?' And she has a flashback to sort of being at her wit's end," Nelson said, which then flows into the question of why she left Jason in the first place. "Cal asks Jason, 'Why did she leave?' And so, well, she didn't know she was pregnant at the time. 'Okay, but why didn't she call you?' 'Well, because I might've told her I didn't want [to be a father].' So you have to just make sure that you can understand both of their perspectives."
The tension from the fact they had a child, and that the two of them were away from their son as you play the game, was significant enough to become a point of discussion within the studio.
"Multiple times throughout development, we had this child, and people are like, 'Can it not be, like, theirs?'" Nelson said. "'Could it be like her little sister? Could it be– because I really feel bad having a good time in Vice City…' and the questions are valid," he continued. "But it wouldn't be powerful if not. So it was up to us to figure out how [you resolve that tension]."
Like with Lucia's justification for leaving Tomás - revealed in a striking dream-slash-flashback later in the game, that mixes typically exaggerated Rockstar satire with genuine social commentary - this then led to a cascade of other character moments. "How about he's in a better position than he would ever be with them, and so he's with her aunt, who's way more stable, and has a loving husband and other little kids, and he's always having a good time?" Nelson said.
He points to a conversation between Jason and Lucia in the game's first chapter as another example. "Very early on, in chapter one, they have this chat where he's like, 'Let's FaceTime - FruitFace is the thing we use - and she's like, 'No, I don't want to confuse him. He's better off until we get our shit together.' And he's like 'Okay' and she's like, 'It's not a cop-out', and he's like, 'No, no, I met my father on the other side of a glass divider, so I'm kind of trying to break that cycle.' And that's right in chapter one, and so it's a thread all the way through."
The result, then, is that the decision to add Tomás ultimately shaped many of the key parts of Jason and Lucia's characters, from their relationships with their own parents – Jason's being in jail, and Lucia's mother too narcissistic to help – to what is, really, the entire narrative arc of the story. And rightly so; a child is a big deal, and one of many reasons why GTA 6 is able to tap into something deeper than any of the previous games in the series before.
